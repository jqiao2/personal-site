The restaurant log
Nine screens, one palette, and the arguments for both.
The decision — light, and here is why
The restaurant log ships light: manila ground, ink brown, terracotta and marigold and avocado. The brief's reference set is a printed object — a laminated menu, a placemat, butcher paper over yesterday's prices — and every one of those is dark ink on cheap warm paper. Rendering that as a dark screen is a photograph of a menu at night, not a menu. The dark version is drawn at the bottom of this sheet so you can see what it costs.
The family resemblance survives because the resemblance was never the hue. It is the anatomy: the same tab nav with the accent underline and the owner's button pinned right, the same 288px sidebar with a two-line wordmark and counts in the right margin, the same section heads with a rule and a See more →, the same Archivo controls and tabular figures, the same ♥ and ↻ marks. Three sections, one hand, three papers.
Three answers, in one paragraph each
The tile is a menu card, and its top band is a slot: a photograph when one exists, and a set plate — price band at 34px over ruled paper, cuisine in small caps — when one doesn't. Same card, same height, same density either way, and the photoless case is the designed case rather than the degraded one. Everything below the band is the menu line you already know: name, cuisine, dot leaders running to a price in the right margin.
The three signals are ranked by what I ask standing on the sidewalk. Verdict is the headline — it is the only one on a tile at a glance and the only one a map pin carries. Stars are metadata, set once in the line under the name. The heart is a marginal mark that appears only when true and never reserves space.
The map opens on New York at neighbourhood scale, because that is where 80% of the pins and 100% of the sidewalk decisions are. The other cities are not hidden: they sit in a rail down the right edge with counts, and they push chevrons against the edge of the viewport pointing at where they actually are.
